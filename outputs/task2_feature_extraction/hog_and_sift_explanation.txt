TASK 2: FEATURE EXTRACTION TECHNICAL EXPLANATION
============================================================

A. WHAT THE HOG VISUALIZATION REPRESENTS:
1. Edge Direction and Local Gradient Vectors:
   - The HOG visualization displays line segments corresponding to dominant gradient directions.
   - The image is divided into small connected spatial regions called 'cells' (16x16 pixels).
   - For each pixel inside a cell, 1-D gradient orientations are compiled into a 9-bin histogram.
2. Brightness / Magnitude Representation:
   - Brighter line segments indicate strong edge boundaries (such as rooflines, building corners,
     and window frames) where gradient magnitude is high.
   - Smooth regions (like the clear sky) have negligible gradient and appear dark.
3. Block Normalization for Photometric Invariance:
   - Overlapping blocks of 2x2 cells normalize local contrast, making the descriptor robust
     against changes in ambient sunlight and shadows.

B. WHY SIFT IS EFFECTIVE FOR IMAGE MATCHING:
1. Scale-Space Extrema Detection (DoG):
   - SIFT uses Difference of Gaussians across multiple octaves to identify points that are
     invariant to zoom and distance changes.
2. Orientation Assignment:
   - One or more canonical orientations are assigned to each keypoint based on local gradient directions,
     ensuring rotation invariance.
3. 128-Dimensional Keypoint Descriptor:
   - 4x4 sub-regions with 8 orientation bins each yield a 128-element descriptor vector,
     providing distinctiveness even when brightness differs between two photographs.
